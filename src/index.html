What am I listening for?

Counterpoint. Each line has its own direction. Listen to the bass first, then add the middle and upper parts. Harmony emerges from the lines together.

Style. French dance rhythms, Italian melodic clarity and German contrapuntal traditions overlap. The filters describe affinities, not borders or exclusive national schools.

The source matters. The six-bar Bach study uses source-aligned voices and notation. Full movements retain the edition's MIDI tracks; a track may contain several musical voices. Other catalog entries link to external scores and recording searches.

Borders change.

The shaded regions are a schematic of territories around 1700, from A. Ourednik and contributors' historical-basemaps. Borders and adjacent snapshot dates are simplified. These colors do not represent musical styles.

Dots identify one city associated with a composer's work. An origin label is a modern description, not an assertion of historical citizenship. Several composers share a city: use the name directory to reach each one.
